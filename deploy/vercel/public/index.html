Visual inspection that measures every defect and sorts every device.

WaferSense finds each IC package in a camera frame, outlines cracks, scratches, contamination, chips and missing pins, measures them in millimetres, and decides whether the part ships, goes to rework or is scrapped. In about 150 ms, on an ordinary CPU.

Inspection decides what ships

Before a packaged chip is taped and shipped, someone checks it under a microscope. That check is slow, tiring and inconsistent, and a missed crack becomes a field failure.

WaferSense automates the visual check. It doesn't stop at "defect or not": it measures each defect against the same written limits an inspector would use, so a hairline scratch passes, a medium one goes to rework and a long one is scrapped.

Every decision comes with the evidence behind it: the outline of the defect, its size in millimetres and the rule it broke.

From camera frame to verdict in four steps

Each step is a separate, replaceable model or module, so a line can retrain one without rebuilding the rest.

  1. Find every device

    A detector locates each IC package in the frame, wherever it lies and at any angle.

    YOLO11n, 416 px

  2. Outline the defects

    Each device is cropped and a segmentation model draws the exact outline of every defect.

    YOLO11n-seg, 5 defect classes

  3. Measure in millimetres

    Outlines become area and length using the camera calibration, so limits are physical, not pixels.

    12 px/mm calibration

  4. Decide

    Written quality limits judge each defect; a device takes the most severe verdict it receives.

    Editable rules file

Five defect types, each with its own limit

Some defects are cosmetic below a size; others scrap the part at any size. The outlines here are the labelled ground truth the models learn from.

Crack

Can spread into the die.

  • any sizeSCRAP

Scratch

Judged by length.

  • below 1.5 mmPASS
  • 1.5 to 4 mmREWORK
  • 4 mm or moreSCRAP

Contamination

Residue, flux or particles, judged by area.

  • below 0.5 mm²PASS
  • 0.5 to 3 mm²REWORK
  • 3 mm² or moreSCRAP

Chip

Mould compound broken off an edge.

  • below 0.5 mm²PASS
  • 0.5 mm² or moreSCRAP

Missing pin

The lead can't be soldered.

  • any sizeSCRAP

Kept apart from training

Data is split by camera frame, so no device appears in two splits. Thresholds are tuned on the validation split only.

Scored end to end

A device the detector misses counts as a wrong decision, so the headline number covers the whole pipeline, not one model.

Trained on a CPU

Everything ran on a 2-core CPU with no GPU, using synthetic camera frames with exact defect outlines (750 frames, 1,459 devices).

Checked on real photographs

Synthetic training data needs a reality check. The same design was tested on real transistor photos from the public MVTec AD benchmark, with four real defect types.

An anomaly detector learns only from defect-free parts and highlights anything unusual. A classifier then looks where the heat-map points and names the defect.

How it's built

Open source, reproducible from one command, and small enough to serve from a serverless function.

Vision models

  • YOLO11n detector
  • YOLO11n-seg segmenter
  • U-Net baseline
  • PatchCore anomaly detection
  • YOLO11s-cls defect typing

Training and data

  • PyTorch, Ultralytics
  • Synthetic frame generator with exact masks
  • MVTec AD real-image check
  • 5-fold cross-validation, bootstrap CIs

Serving

  • ONNX Runtime
  • FastAPI on Vercel
  • Browser camera capture

Local tools
